# Module 4: Social Engineering Attacks

## Q1
**Question:** What type of threat allows an attacker to obtain the credentials of a bank client by spoofing the login webpage of a financial institution?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Piggybacking
- Vishing
- Whaling
- Malvertising
**Answer:** Malvertising
**Explanation:** Malvertising involves incorporating malicious ads on trusted websites. Users who click these ads are inadvertently redirected to sites hosting malware, such as a spoofed login page.

## Q2
**Question:** What is a watering hole attack?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- An attack carried out in a phone conversation
- An attack targeted at high-profile business executives and key individuals in a company
- An attack that exploits a website that is commonly accessed by members of a targeted organization
- An attack performed by an unauthorized person who tags along with an authorized person to gain entry to a restricted area
**Answer:** An attack that exploits a website that is commonly accessed by members of a targeted organization
**Explanation:** In a watering hole attack, the attacker profiles websites the intended victim accesses, then scans those websites for possible vulnerabilities.

## Q3
**Question:** What is the act of gaining knowledge or information from a victim without directly asking for that particular information?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Influence
- Elicitation
- Interrogation
- Impersonation
**Answer:** Elicitation
**Explanation:** Elicitation is the act of gaining knowledge or information from people, typically without directly asking for that particular information.

## Q4
**Question:** A threat actor has altered the host file for a commonly accessed website on the computer of a victim. Now when the user clicks on the website link, they are redirected to a malicious website. What type of attack has the threat actor accomplished?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Phishing
- Vishing
- Pharming
- Tailgating
**Answer:** Pharming
**Explanation:** Pharming is an impersonation attack in which a threat actor redirects a victim from a valid website to a malicious one made to appear valid. It can be done by altering the host file, DNS poisoning, or exploiting a DNS server vulnerability.

## Q5
**Question:** Why would a threat actor use the Social-Engineering Toolkit (SET)?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- To send a spear phishing email
- To spoof a phone number
- To manipulate users by leveraging XSS vulnerabilities
- To practice social engineering elicitation, interrogation, and pretexting skills
**Answer:** To send a spear phishing email
**Explanation:** SET is a tool used to launch numerous social engineering attacks and can integrate with third-party tools and frameworks such as Metasploit. It can be used to create spear phishing emails easily.

## Q7
**Question:** A salesperson is attempting to convince a customer to buy a product because limited supplies are available. Which social engineering method of influence is being used by the salesperson?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Social proof
- Authority
- Likeness
- Scarcity
**Answer:** Scarcity
**Explanation:** Scarcity creates a feeling of urgency in a decision-making context, and salespeople often use it to manipulate clients.

## Q8
**Question:** What method of influence is characterized when a celebrity endorses a product on social media?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Social proof
- Scarcity
- Authority
- Fear
**Answer:** Social proof
**Explanation:** Social proof is used when an individual can't determine the appropriate behavior and instead follows what others appear to be doing. Social engineers can manipulate multiple people at once with this technique.

## Q9
**Question:** Apple is a company constantly working towards making its products and processes more environmentally friendly. Therefore, the Apple brand is associated with ideals and values that customers can relate to and support. What method of influence is being used by Apple?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Fear
- Scarcity
- Authority
- Likeness
**Answer:** Likeness
**Explanation:** Likeness is a method of influence in which things or people influence individuals they like — most people like what is aesthetically or ideologically appealing.

## Q10
**Question:** A threat actor has sent a phishing email to a victim stating that suspicious activity has been detected on their bank account and that they must immediately click on a provided link to change their password. What method of influence is being used by the threat actor?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Social proof
- Authority
- Likeness
- Urgency
**Answer:** Urgency
**Explanation:** The email appeared to come from a bank (an authoritative body to the victim) and created a perception of urgency and scarcity of time, increasing the likelihood the victim would act quickly.

## Q11
**Question:** Which social engineering physical attack statement is correct?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- In the tailgating attack, an unauthorized person tags along with an authorized person to gain entry to a restricted area with the person's consent.
- In the piggybacking attack, an unauthorized person tags along with an authorized person to gain entry to a restricted area without the person's consent.
- Badge cloning attacks cannot be performed by software.
- Shoulder surfing can be prevented by using special screen filters for computer displays.
**Answer:** Shoulder surfing can be prevented by using special screen filters for computer displays.
**Explanation:** With piggybacking, an unauthorized person tags along with an authorized person's consent; tailgating is similar but usually occurs without consent. Badge cloning attacks can be performed with specialized software and hardware. Special screen filters can prevent someone from viewing a display at an angle, mitigating shoulder surfing.

## Q12
**Question:** Which tool provides a threat actor a web console to manipulate users who are victims of cross-site scripting (XSS) attacks?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Asterisk
- SET
- BeEF
- Nikto
**Answer:** BeEF
**Explanation:** Browser Exploitation Framework (BeEF) manipulates users by leveraging XSS vulnerabilities, starting a web service (port 3000 by default) that gives the attacker a web console to manipulate XSS victims.

- Asterisk
- BeEF

## Q14
**Question:** What two physical attacks are mitigated by using access control vestibules? (Choose two.)
**Type:** multi
**Options:**
- Shoulder surfing
- Dumpster diving
- Tailgating
- Badge cloning
- Piggybacking
**Answer:** Tailgating; Piggybacking
**Explanation:** An access control vestibule (formerly a mantrap) is a small space fitting usually one person, with two sets of closely spaced doors where the first must close before the second opens — defeating piggybacking and tailgating.

## Q15
**Question:** Which two access control options are commonly used in conjunction with access control vestibules? (Choose two.)
**Type:** multi
**Options:**
- Proximity card and PIN
- Turnstile
- Security guard
- Toll collector
- Biometric scan
**Answer:** Proximity card and PIN; Biometric scan
**Explanation:** Multifactor authentication is often used with an access control vestibule — for example, a proximity card and PIN may be required at the first door and a biometric scan at the second.

## Q16
**Question:** Which resource would mitigate piggybacking and tailgating?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Security guard
- Camera
- "No trespassing" warnings
- Badge/card access
**Answer:** Security guard
**Explanation:** Turnstiles, double entry doors, and security guards can eliminate piggybacking and tailgating, though they are generally less expensive and less effective than access control vestibules.

**Type:** single
**Options:**
- BeEF

**Explanation:** The Social-Engineer Toolkit (SET) launches numerous social engineering attacks and integrates with frameworks such as Metasploit. It's installed by default in Kali Linux and Parrot Security.

## Q18
**Question:** Who is the target of a whaling attack?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Upper managers such as the CEO or key individuals in an organization
- Ordinary users
- User groups of social networks such as Facebook and Twitter
- Companies that use animals in product testing
**Answer:** Upper managers such as the CEO or key individuals in an organization
**Explanation:** Whaling targets a company's high-profile executives and key individuals, aiming to steal sensitive information or compromise the victim's system to then target other high-profile victims.

## Q19
**Question:** What is the purpose of a vishing attack?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- To create emails and web pages to collect sensitive information from a user
- To convince a victim on a phone call to disclose private or financial information
- To use text messages to send malware or malicious links to mobile devices of users
- To use USB sticks to compromise the systems of victims
**Answer:** To convince a victim on a phone call to disclose private or financial information
**Explanation:** Vishing is a social engineering attack carried out over a phone conversation, where the attacker persuades the victim to reveal private, personal, or financial information — typically to steal identity or financial data.

## Q24
**Question:** A new employee is celebrating their position with a large company by posting a picture of their access identification on social media. What kind of physical attack has the new employee unknowingly enabled?

## Q25
**Question:** A user has found a USB pen drive in the corporate parking lot. What should the user do with this pen drive?
**Type:** single
**Options:**
- Throw the pen drive away
- Deliver the pen drive to the security sector of the company
- Plug the pen drive into a computer of the company, try to delete all the files, and use the pen drive for personal use
- Plug the pen drive into a computer of the company, try to access the files to identify who the pen drive belongs to
**Answer:** Deliver the pen drive to the security sector of the company
**Explanation:** USB drop key attacks involve leaving USB devices in strategic locations for victims to find and plug in, unknowingly installing malware. The safest action is to deliver the found drive to the company's security team rather than inserting it into any system.
